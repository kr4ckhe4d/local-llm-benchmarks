# R9700 + RX 9070 XT — what a second card bought

Measured 2026-10-05, after a Radeon AI PRO R9700 (32 GB) went in beside the
RX 9070 XT (16 GB).

**This file does not replace [README.md](README.md)** or
[ddr5-9800x3d.md](ddr5-9800x3d.md). Those are the single-card record and stay
as measured. What follows is what the second card changed, measured the same
way, and why every preset in `models-preset.ini` moved. The raw material and
scripts are in [`benchmarks/raw/r9700-two-cards/`](benchmarks/raw/r9700-two-cards/)
(the original logs were lost to a reboot). Every figure here was then rerun
with captured output, in [`logs/`](benchmarks/raw/r9700-two-cards/logs/):
Qwen3.8 and Gemma 4 the same night, the rest on 2026-10-06. All of them
reproduce the first session to within 2%, with one exception noted below.

**Build.** Everything above the 2026-10-07 additions ran on llama.cpp 11345
(`a868c3e3c`). The router moved to b11434 (`5e03bdd87`) on 2026-10-06; that
build was measured against 11345 first, preset by preset, in
[llama-b11434.md](llama-b11434.md) (flat apart from Laguna +4%, GLM +5%,
gpt-oss-20b −3.6%, Qwen3.8 128K prefill −4%). Rows added later say which build
they ran on.

**The short version.** MoE models stop offloading experts to the CPU and gain
38-183%, and their speed no longer depends on the context *size* (filled to
128K they still slow to between a fifth and a half, see "At real 128K depth").
Qwen3.8-27B runs a better file with MTP at every context, 71 tok/s, and triples
generation at 128K depth. A dense model that already fit gains nothing from the second card
except room.

## What changed

| | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| GPU | RX 9070 XT, 16,304 MiB | **+ AI PRO R9700, 32,624 MiB** (48,928 total) |
| PCIe | 9070 XT x16 Gen5 | **both x8 Gen5**, CPU root ports 00:01.1 and 00:01.3 |
| CPU, RAM | 9800X3D, 32 GB DDR5-6000 | *unchanged* |
| ROCm | `7.2.53211-3d9ef42` | *unchanged* |
| llama.cpp | build 11345 (`a868c3e3c`) | *unchanged* |
| GGUFs | — | *unchanged* (plus Qwen3.8 Q8_0, already on disk for KLD) |

Both cards are Navi 48, `gfx1201`: same die, same ~640 GB/s of memory
bandwidth. The R9700 has twice the memory and a 300 W limit against the
9070 XT's 330.

### The slot link is x8, whatever the card says

Each Navi 48 card has a PCIe switch on board, and the link from that switch to
the GPU always reports x16 Gen5. `current_link_width` on the GPU device reads
that internal link. The real slot link is one hop up:

```
9070 XT  03:00.0 x16  <- 02:00.0 x16  <- 01:00.0 x8  <- 00:01.1 x8 (CPU)
R9700    07:00.0 x16  <- 06:00.0 x16  <- 05:00.0 x8  <- 00:01.3 x8 (CPU)
```

The board bifurcates its CPU lanes x8/x8. That is ~32 GB/s each way per card,
plenty for layer split, which moves only activations between cards. It would
matter for row split (`-sm row`), which was not tried.

### Three tools, three numberings

| | DRM (`/sys/class/drm`) | llama.cpp | nvtop |
|---|---|---|---|
| RX 9070 XT | card1 | ROCm0 | Device 2 |
| AI PRO R9700 | card0 | ROCm1 | Device 1 |
| 9800X3D iGPU | card2 | ROCm2 | Device 0 |

DRM numbering is assigned at boot, not by slot, and the R9700 took card0. Every
script used to read `card1`, which still happened to be the 9070 XT, so nothing
crashed. It just reported a fraction of a split model's memory. `-ts` and
`--device` order follows the llama.cpp column. Name cards, never index them.

The iGPU is still enumerated and still unused, as ddr5-9800x3d.md found: a
process opens a context on it and allocates nothing.

## How llama.cpp uses two cards

With no flags, llama.cpp splits layers across both cards in proportion to their
memory. The R9700 gets about two thirds:

| | R9700 | 9070 XT |
|---|---|---|
| Qwen3.8-27B IQ4_XS, 32K | 10,885 MiB | 5,479 MiB |
| Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, 128K, no offload | 16,297 MiB | 9,706 MiB |

**The 9070 XT is always the tight card.** It gets a third of the layers but also
carries the desktop (~550 MiB), and on every config measured here it ran out of
headroom first. The summed free figure is misleading for a split: Qwen3.6 at
128K has 22.7 GB free across both cards and 6.5 GB on the 9070 XT, and the
9070 XT is the one that fails.

**Generation does not get faster from a split alone.** Layer split runs the
cards one after the other for each token, and both have the same bandwidth.
Qwen3.8-27B IQ4_XS at 32K, which fit on one card already, went from 30.27 to
31.57 tok/s, +4%.

**Prefill is pipelined, and the R9700 is the bottleneck.** With `-b 2048` and a
smaller `-ub`, llama.cpp overlaps micro-batches across the cards. During a
128K-token prefill nvtop showed the R9700 at 100% and its 300 W cap, 2,776 MHz,
while the 9070 XT averaged ~50% at 205 W. The card with two thirds of the
layers does two thirds of the work and sets the pace. An even `-ts` could keep
both busy and might buy up to a third more prefill. **Not yet measured.**

## Measuring two cards

`benchmarks/gpu-mem.sh` (commit `4c4b695`) is now sourced by `fit.sh`,
`run-suite.sh`, `kld-test.sh` and `switch-model.sh`. It finds discrete cards by
size (over 2 GiB, which drops the iGPU), sums VRAM and GTT across them, and
names cards from their PCI device ID.

`fit.sh` polls each card's peak as well as the summed peak, and adds a line:

```
ctx=131072  model=25594  peak=26203  free=22725  (total=48928, base=609, ...)
      per-card:  R9700 model=16338 peak=16395 free=16229  9070XT model=9256 peak=9808 free=6496  | tightest: 9070XT 6496 free
```

**Judge a split config by the tightest card.** Everything below uses a 1 GiB
margin on it, because the 9070 XT drives the desktop, and desktop VRAM moved by
up to ~700 MiB within one session on the single-card box.

‡ **Laguna Q8 256k as written does not hold its speed.** That preset keeps 34
Q8 expert layers in host memory. Its three runs read 35.95, 25.62 and 19.54:
the first matches the first session's 36.17, and each later run is slower. The
gain is against the first run. The likely cause is host-memory pressure on a
32 GB box, but it was not traced. It does not touch the shipped `-ncmoe 2`
config, which held at 76.1-76.3.

* **The more a preset offloaded, the more it gains**, which is the
  ddr5-9800x3d.md mechanism run to its end: expert offload was bounded by the
  ~53 GB/s the 9800X3D's single CCD can pull from RAM. Remove the offload and
  that ceiling leaves the path entirely.
* **Speed is now flat with context size.** Each model runs at the same rate
  from 32K to 256K, because the longer contexts no longer pay for themselves
  with more CPU layers. This is the 700-token probe in an *empty* context of
  each size. Filled to 128K, generation drops to 20-51% of these figures
  (measured 2026-10-07, "At real 128K depth" below).
* **The old presets were already slower on two cards than on one.** qwen3.6 32k
  measured 53.02 here against 58.12 on the single card (ddr5-9800x3d.md).
  Splitting layers across cards while experts sit on the CPU adds hops. Do not
  bring `n-cpu-moe` back on a two-card preset without measuring it again.
* **Q8 is no longer the slow option.** Gemma Q8 costs 8% against Q4 (it cost
  47%), and Laguna Q8 runs at 89% of Q4. Gemma's Q4 turns out to sit close to
  its Q8 (see "Open"), so Q4 stays its default.

## Loading: mmap does not survive 32 GB of RAM

The first sweep timed out on Laguna Q8 (35.6 GB): `fit.sh` waited 300 s and
llama-server never reached "listening on", with IO pressure at 52%. The file is
bigger than the box's RAM, so loading it through mmap thrashes the page cache,
the same failure that made the BF16 KLD reference impractical (gsq-rco.md).

This build replaced `--no-mmap` with `--load-mode`, and `dio` reads with
DirectIO, bypassing the page cache:

| | First load | Second load |
|---|---|---|
| Qwen3.6 22 GB, mmap | 5.6 s | **1.8 s** (warm cache) |
| Qwen3.6 22 GB, dio | 3.8 s | 3.8 s |
| Laguna Q8 35.6 GB, mmap | > 300 s, timed out | — |
| Laguna Q8 35.6 GB, dio | **10 s** | — |

mmap wins only on a warm cache, which the router rarely has when it switches
models on 32 GB. `[*]` now sets `load-mode = dio` for every preset: at most 2 s
slower, and never a timeout.

**One catch.** `--lazy-mode`, which reads huge lookup tables (per-layer
embeddings) row by row from disk instead of loading them, requires mmap. A
preset that needs it must set `load-mode = mmap` itself.

draft acceptance identical. Free memory on the 9070 XT reads ~250 MiB lower on
every config. Whatever took it, it was outside these runs (`base` was 610 MiB
both times); drift like this is what the 1 GiB margin is for. The first of each three
MTP runs always reads lower (~1 point of acceptance, ~0.7 tok/s), so the median
is the figure to quote.

faithful of the old files (KLD 0.0179 against Q3_K_XL-v3's 0.0263). Q8_0 is not
KLD-scored against BF16, but the Q8_0 reference in `kld-test.sh` reproduces the
BF16 numbers to within 1.5% (gsq-rco.md), so it is close to lossless.

Presets now: `qwen3.8-27B-32k`, `-128k`, `-256k` on IQ4_XS + MTP, and
`qwen3.8-27B-q8-32k`, `-q8-128k` on Q8_0 + MTP. The old names are aliases
(16k-mtp, 32k-mtp and 32k route to 32k; 64k routes to 128k), checked through
the router.

Prefill tok/s / generation tok/s. Q4 and Q8 read 154.5 tok/s at 128K alike,
so the weights are not it. Prefill time roughly quadruples per doubling of
depth: attention dominates, and costs GLM far more than its peers. KV type
and `-ub` barely move prefill; `-fa off` is far worse and cannot run at 128K
at all (an 11 GB score buffer on the 9070 XT; at 64K both cards ran nearly
full, so its 32K figure may include memory pressure). Q8 itself ran 70.3 tok/s
in an empty context against Q4's 78.5. GLM-4.7-Flash is MLA attention with 20
query heads per KV head; upstream's Vulkan backend has a known flash-attention
slow path for that ratio (llama.cpp #29965), and something similar on ROCm is
the likely cause, not verified. No flag fixes it here. Qwen3.6 is a hybrid
with 10 of 40 layers carrying KV, so it is a flattering control; Gemma 4
(1,203) and Laguna (2,997) at 128K are the fairer comparison.

## What this means

* **The binding constraint moved from VRAM to the 9070 XT's share of it.** It
  holds a third of every model plus the desktop. A future fit question is
  "does the 9070 XT keep 1 GiB", and `fit.sh` now answers it directly.
* **For MoE models the second card is a different machine.** The host-memory
  ceiling that ddr5-9800x3d.md spent a file measuring is out of the path.
* **For dense models it is room, not speed.** Better files, MTP and q8_0 KV
  everywhere, and long contexts at full quality. The speed comes from MTP,
  which the room made possible.

## Open

* **Even split for prefill.** `-ts 1,1` where half the model fits the 9070 XT
  (all Qwen3.8 IQ4_XS presets do). Possible upside: the third of prefill time
  the 9070 XT now spends waiting.
* **Gemma 4 Q8 vs Q4: measured, and Q4 is about as faithful as Qwen3.8
  IQ4_XS.** `kld-test.sh` cannot do it: `llama-perplexity` puts Gemma 4 Q8_0

## Reproducing

```bash
cd benchmarks
MODEL=Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-UD-Q4_K_M.gguf ./fit.sh 131072 --load-mode dio -ub 1024 -b 2048 -fa on -ctk q8_0 -ctv q8_0
DIO=1 python3 raw/r9700-two-cards/moe_sweep.py /tmp/sweep.txt laguna-33B-A3B-q8-256k
./raw/r9700-two-cards/dense.sh qwen38
./raw/r9700-two-cards/depth.sh
LOGDIR=raw/r9700-two-cards/logs ./raw/r9700-two-cards/depth_presets.sh qwen3.6-35B-A3B-128k ...
./raw/r9700-two-cards/logs/run-q6.sh
```

`moe_sweep.py` sweeps only presets that set `n-cpu-moe` and treats that value
as "as written", so against today's file it touches laguna-q8-256k alone. To
reproduce the whole table, point it at the single-card presets without touching
the live file the router reads:

```bash
git show 4c4b695:models-preset.ini > /tmp/old.ini
PRESETS=/tmp/old.ini DIO=1 python3 benchmarks/raw/r9700-two-cards/moe_sweep.py /tmp/sweep.txt
```

The router must be stopped first: `fit.sh` refuses to measure beside another
`llama-server`.
