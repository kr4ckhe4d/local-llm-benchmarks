## Why only 31 tok/s (2026-11-21)
Same file, same R9700, Vulkan (RADV, build-vk of the same worktree) vs ROCm, llama-bench -fa 1 -lm dio -r 3:

| backend | pp512          | tg128        |
| ------- | -------------: | -----------: |
| Vulkan1 | 629.65 ± 8.22  | 27.00 ± 0.02 |
| ROCm1   | 825.34 ± 2.81  | 30.98 ± 0.12 |

Vulkan is slower, so ROCm is not the cause. The cause is the graph: test-export-graph-ops
(graph-ops.txt) reports 12,178 nodes per token, 4,150 of them views, so ~8,000 kernels per
generated token (48 layer passes from the 3x loop over layers 6-18, each with shortconv,
relative attention, attention-residual and loop-gate ops on 2-5 element tensors).
test-backend-ops perf on ROCm1 (op-perf-rocm.txt; it hit a GPU memory fault after 240 of 310
ops, all generation-shape matmuls finished) puts every tiny op at ~2.9-3.4 us. The weight
matmuls run at bandwidth: 8-expert MUL_MAT_ID 21 us NVFP4 down (~570 GB/s of active
weights), 28 us NVFP4 gate_up, 65 / 150 us BF16 down / gate_up (~570-660 GB/s); the 201K
output head 1.6 ms (539 GB/s). Summed, weight reads are ~8 ms of the 32 ms per token; the
other ~24 ms is ~8,000 small kernels at ~3 us each. Getting past ~30 tok/s needs op fusion
in llama.cpp's inkling2_loop graph, not a different backend or quant.

Follow-up (same day): with fewer kernels per token and CUDA/HIP fusions on a local llama.cpp branch,
tg128 on the R9700 went 31.0 -> 54.0 tok/s, pp512 825 -> 876. See ../../inkling-2-mini-speedups.md and
speedups/ (final throughput, build-equivalence KLD, test results and the 16 patches).
